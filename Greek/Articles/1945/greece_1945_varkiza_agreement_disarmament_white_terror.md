## English — Summary

The February 1945 Varkiza Agreement ended the Dekemvriana and required ELAS disarmament, but unequal security, contested amnesty and escalating right-wing violence undermined the settlement.

## English — Full Story

- Background — The liberation of Greece was followed by confrontation between the EAM-ELAS movement, the British-backed government and rival armed formations. Fighting in Athens ended without resolving control of the state or public security.

- Central episode — Signed on 12 February 1945, the agreement provided for disarmament, political liberties, a plebiscite and elections, and an amnesty that excluded undefined 'common crimes'.

- Immediate response — ELAS surrendered weapons, but state and para-state violence, arrests and persecution of leftists grew in many areas. Mutual distrust destroyed confidence in peaceful political competition.

- Human and institutional impact — The failure to provide equal security and credible accountability contributed to the cycle known as the White Terror and helped create conditions for the Greek Civil War.

- Accountability — The settlement must be evaluated through implementation: disarmament, policing, prosecutions, protection of political rights, British influence and the capacity of institutions to restrain armed supporters.

- Legacy — Varkiza is a classic case of a peace agreement whose formal provisions could not survive asymmetric coercion and the absence of trusted institutions.
