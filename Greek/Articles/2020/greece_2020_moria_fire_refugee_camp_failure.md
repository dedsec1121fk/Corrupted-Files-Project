## English — Summary

Fires in September 2020 destroyed the overcrowded Moria reception center on Lesvos and left roughly twelve thousand asylum seekers without shelter, revealing long-standing failures in Greek and European reception policy.

## English — Full Story

- Background — Moria held far more people than its designed capacity. Residents faced inadequate sanitation, insecurity, long asylum delays and COVID-19 restrictions, while local communities and institutions were under severe pressure.

- Central episode — A series of fires from 8 to 10 September destroyed almost all accommodation in the formal center and adjacent Olive Grove area.

- Immediate response — Thousands slept outdoors before authorities created an emergency site at Kara Tepe with support from UNHCR and other organizations. Transfers, testing and shelter allocation occurred under police control and urgent humanitarian pressure.

- Human and institutional impact — Families, children, pregnant women, elderly people and people with disabilities lost shelter during a pandemic. The crisis exposed the consequences of containment, overcrowding and shared European responsibility.

- Accountability — Responsibility extends beyond the suspected ignition of individual fires to capacity decisions, asylum delays, camp design, public health, security, EU policy and the failure to act on repeated warnings.

- Legacy — Moria became an international symbol of the humanitarian costs of deterrence-based migration policy and of temporary emergency sites that risk becoming long-term institutions.
