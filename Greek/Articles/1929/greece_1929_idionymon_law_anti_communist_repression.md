## English — Summary

Law 4229/1929, known as the Idionymon, criminalized forms of political organization aimed at overthrowing the social order and became a major instrument against communists and militant labor activism.

## English — Full Story

- Background — Interwar Greece faced refugee integration, labor conflict, fear of revolutionary movements and unstable institutions. The Venizelos government sought a legal mechanism to contain communist organization.

- Central episode — The law punished efforts to apply ideas aimed at the violent overthrow of the existing social system and enabled imprisonment, exile and restrictions on political activity.

- Immediate response — Police and courts used the measure against communists, organizers and strikers. Its broad language helped turn political belief, association and labor conflict into security questions.

- Human and institutional impact — The law narrowed civil liberties, contributed to surveillance and exile practices and created legal continuity with harsher anti-communist systems under later authoritarian regimes.

- Accountability — The case tests the responsibility of elected institutions when they construct repressive powers that later governments can expand beyond the original political context.

- Legacy — The Idionymon is a key precedent in the history of Greek anti-communism and illustrates how exceptional security law can become durable political infrastructure.
