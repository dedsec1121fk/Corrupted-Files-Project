## English — Summary

The 1909 Goudi movement was a military intervention that pressured Greece's political system for administrative, military and constitutional reform without immediately replacing parliamentary government.

## English — Full Story

- Background — Military dissatisfaction after the 1897 defeat, frustration with royal influence and broader demands for state modernization created a climate in which the Military League gained leverage.

- Central episode — In August 1909 officers gathered at Goudi and issued demands affecting the armed forces and government. Their pressure destabilized the existing political leadership and encouraged a transition toward reform.

- Immediate response — The League avoided a straightforward seizure of power and sought civilian political mediation. Eleftherios Venizelos emerged as the figure able to channel the crisis into elections and institutional change.

- Human and institutional impact — The movement weakened established parties, changed civil-military relations and accelerated reforms in administration, the armed forces and the constitutional order.

- Accountability — The episode raises the question of how reform can be pursued when armed officers exercise political pressure outside normal constitutional channels, even when they do not form a lasting dictatorship.

- Legacy — Goudi is remembered both as a breach of constitutional normality and as a catalyst for the Venizelist reform era, making its democratic meaning contested.
