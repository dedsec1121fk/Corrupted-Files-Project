Trial of the Six, Asia Minor catastrophe, and punishment politics

Country: Greece
Year: 1922
Category: military disaster / political trial / national trauma
Evidence level: documented historical trial with disputed justice

Full story
----------
After the Asia Minor catastrophe, the Trial of the Six became a dramatic attempt to assign responsibility for defeat, displacement, and national collapse. The case sits between real accountability pressure and the danger of turning a defeated elite into scapegoats under revolutionary conditions.

Confirmed record
----------------
- The trial and executions followed the 1922 military collapse.
- The catastrophe produced mass displacement and a legitimacy crisis.
- The case remains central to Greek debates over responsibility and due process.

Disputed or unresolved points
-----------------------------
- Whether the trial delivered justice or emergency vengeance remains debated.
- Military, diplomatic, royalist, Venizelist, and Allied factors must be separated.

Conspiracy theory / rumor lane
------------------------------
- Some theories say the trial hid deeper foreign and elite responsibility; wider responsibility is plausible, but hidden-master claims need documents.
- The structural reading is that national disasters create demand for visible punishment faster than careful truth.

Reader warning
--------------
The theory lane is included because rumors and conspiracy claims shape public memory. It is not an endorsement; the reader should privilege primary records, official findings, court files, and named investigations.
