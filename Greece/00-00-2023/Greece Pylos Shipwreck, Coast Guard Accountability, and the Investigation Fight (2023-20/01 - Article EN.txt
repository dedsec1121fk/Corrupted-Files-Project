Status: [DOCUMENTED / STRONG EVIDENCE]

Summary:
The Pylos/Adriana shipwreck became one of the most important modern Greek accountability cases because it sits at the junction of migration control, coast guard power, institutional self-protection, and the struggle to establish an accurate factual record after mass death at sea.

Why it matters:
This is not just a migration story. It is a state-accountability story. When hundreds die under prolonged official monitoring and the facts remain contested for years, distrust grows not only because of the disaster itself but because of the fight over the truth afterward.

What is proved:
- Reuters reported that Greece's Ombudsman recommended disciplinary action against coast guard officers and found clear indications of fault in the handling of the case.
- Reuters reported that the Ombudsman's inquiry found the coast guard breached maritime rules, failed to escalate the situation into distress soon enough, and delayed search-and-rescue steps while waiting for the vessel to move on.
- Reuters reported in May 2025 that a Greek naval court charged 17 coast guard officers over the shipwreck.
- Reuters reported in November 2025 that four more senior officers, including the current chief, were prosecuted, bringing the total charged to 21.
- AP reported that rights groups and UN agencies continued calling for a fuller, credible investigation.

What this archive does NOT claim:
- It does NOT claim every survivor account has been forensically proven in court.
- It does NOT claim final criminal liability has been settled. The judicial process is still unfolding.
