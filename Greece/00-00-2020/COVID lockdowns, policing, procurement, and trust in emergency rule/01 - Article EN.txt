COVID lockdowns, policing, procurement, and trust in emergency rule

Country: Greece
Year: 2020
Category: pandemic governance / emergency powers / procurement
Evidence level: documented emergency policy with contested proportionality and procurement questions

Full story
----------
The COVID period in Greece joined real public-health danger with lockdown enforcement, SMS movement permissions, hospital capacity debates, direct awards, expert committees, media campaigns, and distrust over whether emergency tools were proportionate.

Confirmed record
----------------
- Greece used lockdowns, movement controls, and emergency public-health measures.
- Procurement and media funding questions became part of the trust debate.
- Hospital pressure and mortality data shaped public judgment.

Disputed or unresolved points
-----------------------------
- Some measures were medically justified; others are debated for proportionality and enforcement inequality.
- Procurement criticism requires contract-level evidence.

Conspiracy theory / rumor lane
------------------------------
- The conspiracy lane treats every pandemic measure as a control plot; the documented record supports a narrower audit of emergency powers, money, and transparency.
- The structural reading is that emergency governance can outlive the emergency if oversight is weak.

Source trail
------------

- Procurement transparency portals
