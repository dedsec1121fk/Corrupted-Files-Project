GREECE: Petsas List, Media Funding, and Message Control (2020-2026)

Status: [DOCUMENTED] for the state-funded COVID media campaign, distribution controversy, information-access litigation, and recurring rule-of-law criticism around transparency.
Status: [ALLEGED / OVERSTATED] for claims that every Greek media outlet is directly bought and centrally controlled by one payment list.

Summary:
The so-called "Petsas List" became a shorthand for fears of message control through public money:
- In 2020, the Greek government ran a "Stay at Home" campaign during COVID-19 and distributed substantial public advertising funds across media outlets.
- Journalists, watchdogs, and rights groups challenged the opacity of the distribution criteria and the reluctance to disclose underlying documents.
- Later reporting and rights-group documentation treated the affair as part of a broader pattern: concentrated media ownership, weak transparency, and pressure on independent journalism.

Why it matters:
This is one of the clearest Greek examples of how state advertising can become a pressure point. Direct censorship is not the only way to shape the media environment.

What this archive does NOT claim:
- It does NOT claim every funded outlet was bribed in a criminal sense.
- It does NOT claim the campaign itself was fictional.
- It does claim that the allocation controversy became a real transparency and media-freedom scandal.
