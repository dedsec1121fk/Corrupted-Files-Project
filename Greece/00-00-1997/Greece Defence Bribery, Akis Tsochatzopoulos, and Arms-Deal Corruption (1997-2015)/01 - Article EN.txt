Greece: Defence Bribery, Akis Tsochatzopoulos, and Arms-Deal Corruption (1997-2015)

1) Kickbacks around arms deals became one of modern Greece's most notorious corruption symbols
Status: [DOCUMENTED]
Summary:
The Akis Tsochatzopoulos case became one of the clearest Greek examples of defence-sector corruption at elite level.
Reuters reported that Tsochatzopoulos, who served as defence minister from 1996 to 2001, was convicted of putting kickbacks from arms deals between 1997 and 2001 into foreign bank accounts. The case was widely treated as a symbol of the graft that plagued crisis-era Greece.
Reuters also reported in 2015 that Greece sought more than 100 million euros in compensation from German defence firms over alleged bribes connected to helicopters and submarine contracts, and that Greece hoped for much more across a broader series of cases.

Why it matters:
This case connects multiple themes people often “connect dots” around:
- large defence budgets
- opaque procurement
- offshore money flows
- political protection
- and ordinary taxpayers being told to accept austerity while corruption in big contracts surfaces later

Key details:
- Reuters reported that Tsochatzopoulos received a 20-year sentence for money laundering tied to kickbacks from arms deals.
- The same Reuters report described it as the highest-profile conviction of a politician in decades.
- Reuters later reported Greek efforts to seek compensation from German defence firms over alleged bribery in helicopter and submarine contracts.
- The 2015 Reuters report tied the issue directly to the wider debt-crisis environment and Greece's heavy arms spending.

This file does not claim every Greek defence contract was corrupt.
It documents one of the strongest proven cases showing why arms spending and political corruption are often linked in public distrust.
