Halyvourgia strike, riot police intervention, and austerity labor conflict

Country: Greece
Year: 2012
Category: labor conflict / austerity / policing
Evidence level: documented strike and police intervention with contested labor narratives

Full story
----------
The Halyvourgia strike became a crisis-era labor file about wage cuts, employer pressure, industrial decline, police intervention, and whether austerity turned labor disputes into public-order operations.

Confirmed record
----------------
- The strike lasted for months during the austerity period.
- Riot police intervened to reopen the plant.
- The dispute became a symbol for labor rights under crisis pressure.

Disputed or unresolved points
-----------------------------
- Economic viability, union strategy, and employer responsibility were argued intensely.
- The legal right to work and the right to strike were framed against each other.

Conspiracy theory / rumor lane
------------------------------
- Some theories describe coordinated state-employer strikebreaking; documented police intervention is clear, but coordination claims need records.
- The structural reading is that austerity made police power available to settle economic conflict.

Source trail
------------
- Greek labor reporting
- Court and police-intervention public record
- Union and employer statements

Reader warning
--------------
The theory lane is included because rumors and conspiracy claims shape public memory. It is not an endorsement; the reader should privilege primary records, official findings, court files, and named investigations.
