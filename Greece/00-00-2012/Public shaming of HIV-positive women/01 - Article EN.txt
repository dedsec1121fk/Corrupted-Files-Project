Public shaming of HIV-positive women
====================================

Country: Greece
Year folder: 2012
Category: public health / police spectacle
Evidence level: documented rights scandal

Core story
----------
Greek authorities published photos and identities of HIV-positive women accused in a public-
health panic.

What happened, in full context
------------------------------
This case shows how public health can become police theater. Vulnerable women were treated
as threats to respectable society. Later legal and rights criticism exposed the cruelty of
using disease, sex-work stigma and media exposure as political proof of toughness.

Timeline
--------
- 2012: arrests and public exposure.
- Afterward: legal and rights backlash.
- Long-term: case remains a public-health ethics warning.

Key facts and evidence points
-----------------------------
- Women’s identities and photos were published.
- The case occurred before 2012 elections.
- Rights groups condemned the practice.

Why this case matters
---------------------
It belongs in the database because it combines body control, shame and state publicity.

Competing explanations and caution
-----------------------------------
Authorities framed it as health protection; critics saw rights abuse and scapegoating.

Source trail / suggested verification
-------------------------------------
