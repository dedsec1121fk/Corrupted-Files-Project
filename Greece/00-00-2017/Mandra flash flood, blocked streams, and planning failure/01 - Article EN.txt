Mandra flash flood, blocked streams, and planning failure

Country: Greece
Year: 2017
Category: disaster / urban planning / accountability
Evidence level: documented disaster with official and judicial review

Full story
----------
The 2017 Mandra flood killed residents and exposed how illegal construction, blocked streams, weak drainage, and delayed flood works can turn weather into an accountability case. The scandal was not that rain fell; it was that known vulnerabilities met a state that had normalized risk.

Confirmed record
----------------
- Twenty-four people died in the disaster.
- Investigations and court proceedings examined local, regional, and planning responsibilities.
- The public record repeatedly focused on flood channels, unauthorized structures, and delayed public works.

Disputed or unresolved points
-----------------------------
- Responsibility is distributed across decades, administrations, property choices, and emergency response decisions.
- The exact share of fault between natural intensity and institutional neglect remains debated.

Conspiracy theory / rumor lane
------------------------------
- Rumors claim protected construction interests were deliberately shielded for votes and money; some local incentives are credible, but a single master plan is not proven.
- The stronger reading is systemic tolerance: many small permissions and delays created one large disaster.

Why it matters
--------------
It matters because climate risk becomes institutional failure when known hazards are mapped but not fixed.

Source trail
------------

Reader warning
--------------
This record deliberately separates documented facts from allegations and conspiracy theories. The theory lane is included because those claims shape public memory, not because every claim is proven.
