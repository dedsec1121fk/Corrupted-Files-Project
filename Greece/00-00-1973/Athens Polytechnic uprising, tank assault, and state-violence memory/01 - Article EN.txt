Athens Polytechnic uprising, tank assault, and state-violence memory

Country: Greece
Year: 1973
Category: dictatorship / student uprising / state violence
Evidence level: documented uprising and repression with contested memory politics

Full story
----------
The Athens Polytechnic uprising became the central democratic memory file of the Greek dictatorship. Students, workers, radio broadcasts, police repression, the tank at the gate, and later arguments over casualties and ownership of the memory turned the event into a permanent test of truth and legitimacy.

Confirmed record
----------------
- The uprising and military-police repression are documented historical events.
- The tank entry into the Polytechnic became an iconic image of dictatorship violence.
- The event accelerated the regime crisis, even though the dictatorship did not fall immediately that night.

Disputed or unresolved points
-----------------------------
- Casualty lists, responsibility chains, and later commemorative narratives require careful source work.
- Political groups have repeatedly fought over ownership of the memory.

Conspiracy theory / rumor lane
------------------------------
- Denialist theories try to erase deaths or minimize repression; they conflict with the documented record of violence and regime accountability.
- A structural reading is that authoritarian states attack truth after attacking bodies.

Impact map
----------
This case should be read through direct harm, institutional incentives, delayed accountability, and the way later political memory simplifies the record.

Source trail
------------
- Greek historical archives on the Polytechnic uprising
- Court and transition-era accountability material
- Student radio and testimony records

Reader warning
--------------
Theory lanes explain public memory and rumor pressure. They are not proof. Follow primary documents, court records, official findings, named investigations, and technical reports before drawing conclusions.
