Mati wildfire, evacuation failure, and command accountability

Country: Greece
Year: 2018
Category: wildfire disaster / civil protection
Evidence level: documented mass-casualty disaster with contested accountability

Full story
----------
The 2018 Mati wildfire killed 104 people and became one of Greece's defining civil-protection failures. The core questions are operational: warnings, evacuation orders, traffic management, fire-service command, coastal escape routes, and how officials communicated while people were trapped.

Confirmed record
----------------
- The death toll and scale of destruction are documented.
- Official inquiries and trials examined failures in evacuation, coordination, and command.
- The settlement pattern around Mati made escape routes difficult, but emergency decisions still mattered.

Disputed or unresolved points
-----------------------------
- Trials and appeals continue to shape the legal meaning of negligence and duty.
- Public anger also targets political communication during and after the fire, which is harder to measure legally.

Conspiracy theory / rumor lane
------------------------------
- The harshest theory says officials knowingly minimized the disaster for political optics during the first hours; intent is harder to prove than bad information flow and delayed acknowledgment.
- Other theories blame arson networks or land interests; ignition questions should not distract from documented civil-protection failures.

Why it matters
--------------
It matters because a state is judged at the moment citizens need warning, routes, and truthful information.

Source trail
------------
- Greek court proceedings on the Mati fire
- Official fire-service and civil-protection record
- Survivor testimony and investigative reporting

Reader warning
--------------
This record deliberately separates documented facts from allegations and conspiracy theories. The theory lane is included because those claims shape public memory, not because every claim is proven.
