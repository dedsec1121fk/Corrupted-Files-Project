ERT shutdown, black screen, and public broadcasting shock

Country: Greece
Year: 2013
Category: media / austerity governance / state power
Evidence level: documented government shutdown and institutional dispute

Full story
----------
The 2013 shutdown of ERT put a black screen where public broadcasting had been. Supporters framed it as a strike against waste; critics saw an austerity-era act of executive force against public media, workers, archives, regional voice, and democratic continuity.

Confirmed record
----------------
- The government abruptly shut down ERT in June 2013.
- The shutdown produced domestic protests and international criticism.
- A replacement structure and later restoration/reorganization followed.

Disputed or unresolved points
-----------------------------
- The depth of waste and patronage inside ERT is separate from whether a shutdown was proportionate.
- The constitutional and democratic meaning remains debated.

Conspiracy theory / rumor lane
------------------------------
- Some theories say creditors demanded a media blackout as a control experiment; the documented evidence supports austerity pressure, not a proven foreign media-control plot.
- The stronger reading is emergency governance: crisis language made extreme executive action seem normal.

Source trail
------------
- Greek government decisions and court record
- European Broadcasting Union statements
- Contemporaneous Greek media reporting

Reader warning
--------------
This record includes conspiracy theories because they shape public memory. They are explicitly separated from documented anchors and should not be treated as proven unless the source trail supports them.
