Summary:
The Vodafone wiretap scandal is one of the most serious surveillance scandals in modern Greek history.
It involved illegal interception of communications affecting top Greek officials, including the prime minister.
Around the same period, Vodafone employee Costas Tsalikidis was found dead in Athens in March 2005.

Why it matters:
- It is a major Greek proof-of-record case that secret surveillance at the highest levels was real.
- It also shows how a scandal becomes darker when a suspicious or disputed death appears next to it.
- Even when a final murder finding is absent, failures of investigation can become part of the scandal themselves.

What high-quality sources say:
Reuters reported that Tsalikidis was found hanged around the time Vodafone Greece told authorities of widespread wiretapping affecting much of the government, including the prime minister.
Reuters also reported that Greek telecom regulators fined Vodafone over privacy-rule breaches.
The European Court of Human Rights later held that Greece had failed to carry out an effective investigation into the circumstances surrounding Tsalikidis's death.
