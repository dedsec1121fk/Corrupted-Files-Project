Greece: Athens 2004 Olympics, Security Spending, and Debt Narrative (1997-2014) 1) Mega-event pride, heavy spending, and the debate over how much it deepened the crisis path Status: [DOCUMENTED / STRONG EVIDENCE] Summary: The 2004 Athens Olympics remain politically symbolic because they sit at the intersection of prestige, borrowing, infrastructure, and national myth-making. The IMF’s 2005 Greece report said planned deficit reductions reflected the end of Olympics spending worth roughly 1.25% of GDP. Later policy and media discussions argued over how much Olympic spending contributed to the debt path. Reuters reported in 2014 that Greece’s Olympic Committee denied the Games were to blame, while CFR’s debt-crisis timeline states costs exceeded 9 billion euros and borrowing contributed to worsening deficit and debt ratios.
