1) A list of wealthy Greeks with Swiss accounts became a scandal about state protection and selective enforcement
Status: [DOCUMENTED]
Summary:
The “Lagarde List” became one of the clearest Greek crisis-era symbols of elite impunity.
French authorities provided Greece with a list of 2,059 Greeks holding HSBC accounts in Switzerland for possible tax-evasion checks. Instead of becoming a clean major anti-evasion case, the list turned into a scandal about delay, mishandling, disappearance, media prosecution, and political tampering.
Reuters reported that the list was passed from one senior official to another and even got misplaced without apparent action. In 2015, former finance minister George Papaconstantinou was convicted of tampering with the list after the court found that three relatives’ names had been removed.

Why it matters:
The public message was brutal during austerity:
ordinary people were told to accept cuts, taxes, and sacrifice, while a politically explosive list involving wealthy account holders drifted through the state with years of confusion and weak follow-through.

Key details:
- The list contained 2,059 Greeks with HSBC accounts in Switzerland to be checked for possible tax evasion.
- Reuters reported that other countries used the leaked banking data to pursue cases while Greek authorities kept it in drawers.
- Reuters reported that Papaconstantinou was convicted of removing the names of three relatives from the list.
- Reuters also highlighted the outrage over how successive governments failed to fully investigate the people on the list.

This is not proof that every person on the list was guilty.
It is proof that the handling of the list became a scandal of its own, and that the state appeared far more capable of managing public anger than of delivering clean, equal accountability.
