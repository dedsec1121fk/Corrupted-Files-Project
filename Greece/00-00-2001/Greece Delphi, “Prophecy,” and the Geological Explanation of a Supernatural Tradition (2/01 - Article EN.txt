Summary:
The Delphic Oracle is one of the most famous supernatural claims in human history.
What makes it interesting for this archive is not whether the Pythia was “truly psychic,” but that modern geology and geochemistry may explain part of the phenomenon in natural terms.

Scientific American summarized research showing that ancient reports of vapors at Delphi may have had a real basis.
The report says researchers identified geologic faults and hydrocarbon gases in local water, including ethylene, which can produce euphoric or trance-like effects.
A later geology paper abstract likewise argues that gas occurrence and faulting can help explain the ancient accounts.

Why it matters:
- It is a strong example of something treated as supernatural for centuries gaining a plausible scientific explanation.
- It belongs in this archive because it sits exactly at the border between myth, observation, altered states, and geology.
- It is a reminder that some “mysteries” become less mystical once science catches up.

Important caution:
This does NOT prove every detail of every story about Delphi.
It does support the narrower claim that the trance tradition may have been linked to real gases and local geology.
