1. The story
On 6 December 2008, 15-year-old Alexandros Grigoropoulos was shot dead by a police officer in
Exarchia. The killing triggered riots and protests across Greece. The event became more than one
fatal shooting: it symbolized youth anger, police impunity, economic despair, and a generation
that felt the state saw it as a threat before it saw it as citizens.

2. Background
Greece in 2008 was already tense. The coming debt crisis had not fully exploded, but young
people felt blocked by unemployment, corruption, clientelism, and weak institutions. Exarchia
carried its own symbolic charge as a neighborhood of anarchists, students, police confrontation,
and political memory. The shooting struck a society ready to ignite.

3. Chronology and turning points
- 6 December 2008: Alexandros Grigoropoulos was shot in Exarchia.
- Protests and riots spread through Athens and other Greek cities.
- The police officer involved was convicted, and the case passed through later judicial stages.
- The anniversary became a recurring date of protest and memory.
- The event influenced Greek youth politics and policing debates for years.

4. Psychological / social-control angle
The killing became a mirror. For police defenders, the unrest proved disorder. For angry youth,
the shooting proved contempt. For many parents, it proved that a child could become a political
casualty while simply being in the wrong place. The psychological power came from age: a
teenager’s death exposed the emotional distance between authority and the young.

5. What is documented
- Grigoropoulos was shot by a police officer.
- The killing produced mass unrest.
- The officer was convicted.
- The event remains a major symbol in Greek protest culture.

6. What remains disputed, exaggerated, or unresolved
- Debates continue over the riots, property damage, and political exploitation of the death.
- Those debates should not erase the central fact: state violence killed a child.
