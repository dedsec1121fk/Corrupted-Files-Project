Structured bonds, pension funds, and public-money opacity

Country: Greece
Year: 2006
Category: public money / pension funds / financial products
Evidence level: documented financial scandal with disputed responsibility layers

Full story
----------
The structured-bonds scandal asked whether complex financial products were placed into public pension funds through opaque chains of intermediaries. It became a trust case because workers' future income was exposed to deals that ordinary beneficiaries could not inspect.

Confirmed record
----------------
- The scandal involved Greek pension funds and structured financial products.
- Public debate focused on intermediaries, pricing, commissions, and state oversight.
- The case damaged trust in pension-fund governance before the debt crisis.

Disputed or unresolved points
-----------------------------
- Legal responsibility depends on documents, pricing models, approvals, and knowledge of risk.
- Not every complex product is corrupt; opacity and incentives are the key questions.

Conspiracy theory / rumor lane
------------------------------
- The theory lane says pension funds were used as a dumping ground for connected financial deals; that claim must be tested fund by fund.
- A structural reading is that complexity transfers power from beneficiaries to intermediaries.

Impact map
----------
This case should be read through direct harm, institutional incentives, delayed accountability, and the way later political memory simplifies the record.
