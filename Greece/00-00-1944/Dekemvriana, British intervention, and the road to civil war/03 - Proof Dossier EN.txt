Dekemvriana, British intervention, and the road to civil war — PROOF DOSSIER

Verified anchors
----------------
- Armed clashes in Athens after liberation are documented by Greek, British, and international archives.
- British military and political involvement was direct, not merely diplomatic.
- The conflict helped set the path toward the wider Greek Civil War and decades of polarized state memory.

Disputed / weaker evidence
--------------------------
- Responsibility is still argued through ideological lenses: revolution, counterrevolution, foreign control, or security necessity.
- Casualty counts, provocation claims, and command choices require source-by-source reading.

Conspiracy theories to handle carefully
---------------------------------------
- The strongest conspiracy reading says Greece was assigned to a Western sphere before Greeks could decide their future; the documented record supports great-power influence, while total pre-scripted control is harder to prove.
- A structural reading is that armed politics, foreign leverage, and weak institutions made democratic settlement nearly impossible.

Source trail
------------
- British National Archives and wartime diplomatic files
- Greek historical archives on Dekemvriana
- Academic histories of the Greek Civil War

Evidence discipline: prefer court records, official inquiries, archival files, regulator findings, named investigative reporting, and primary documents over anonymous claims or broad hidden-plan narratives.
