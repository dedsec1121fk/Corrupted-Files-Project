Greece: Lambrakis, the parastate, and political murder (1963-1966)

1) A case that became a symbol of state-shadow overlap
Status: [STRONG EVIDENCE / DOCUMENTED CORE]

Summary:
The 1963 assassination of MP and peace activist Grigoris Lambrakis became one of the foundational Greek cases for the idea of a "parakratos" (parastate): violent far-right networks acting with tolerance, assistance, or protection from parts of the security apparatus. The broad historical picture is well established: Lambrakis was attacked after a peace event in Thessaloniki, his death triggered a political crisis, and subsequent investigation exposed links between far-right actors and elements of the police and state machinery.

Why it matters:
- It is one of the clearest Greek examples of the public feeling that official power and unofficial violent networks overlapped.
- It helps explain why later generations of Greeks kept using the word "parakratos."
- It sits between a murder case, a cover-up fear, and a larger crisis of democracy.

Core points:
- Historical and scholarly sources describe the murder as tied to parastate extremists.
- The case triggered mass public reaction and contributed to deeper distrust in institutions before the junta period.
- The identity of every "mastermind" was never cleanly resolved in a way that satisfied public suspicion.

What is proved / what is not:
- PROVED at the core level: Lambrakis was assassinated and the case exposed links between extremists and parts of the security environment.
- STRONG EVIDENCE: the parastate framing is not just literary myth; it is rooted in the investigative and political aftermath.
- NOT PROVED in full: a neat final map of every superior who ordered or protected the crime.
