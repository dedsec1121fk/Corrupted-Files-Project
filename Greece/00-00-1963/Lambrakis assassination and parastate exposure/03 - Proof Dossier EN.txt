- Grigoris Lambrakis was attacked in Thessaloniki in 1963 and died after a political event.
- The investigation exposed links between far-right violence, police tolerance, and parastate networks.
- The central conflict is whether the state merely failed to protect him or tolerated forces around the attack.
- The case matters because it became a symbol of hidden power structures inside a formal democracy.
- It changed Greek political memory and remains a reference point for parastate violence.
- Questions remain about how deep the network reached and how much was officially known.

Evidence reading: strong evidence comes from official records, court proceedings, parliamentary material, regulator documents, named investigations, and contemporaneous reporting. Interpretive claims should be treated as weaker unless they are tied to a concrete document.
