Michalis Kaltezas killing, police impunity, and youth revolt

Country: Greece
Year: 1985
Category: police violence / youth protest / accountability
Evidence level: documented police killing with contested accountability

Full story
----------
The 1985 killing of teenager Michalis Kaltezas by a police officer became a Greek memory file about police violence, Exarchia, youth anger, and whether the justice system treats uniformed violence as ordinary crime or as an exception to be managed politically.

Confirmed record
----------------
- Kaltezas was shot after a Polytechnic anniversary demonstration.
- The case triggered unrest and became a landmark in Greek police-accountability memory.
- Legal proceedings and public debate followed the killing.

Disputed or unresolved points
-----------------------------
- The legal interpretation of intent, threat, and proportionality remained central.
- Political memory often compresses the case into symbols; court records carry the harder detail.

Conspiracy theory / rumor lane
------------------------------
- Some theories say police violence was deliberately tolerated to discipline radical youth; structural tolerance is easier to support than a single secret order.
- The stronger reading is institutional culture: armed authority often receives benefit of doubt denied to protesters.

Source trail
------------
- Greek court and press archives
- Polytechnic anniversary protest records
- Histories of policing and Exarchia

Reader warning
--------------
The theory lane is included because rumors and conspiracy claims shape public memory. It is not an endorsement; the reader should privilege primary records, official findings, court files, and named investigations.
