Apostasia crisis, palace pressure, and parliamentary defections

Country: Greece
Year: 1965
Category: constitutional crisis / palace influence
Evidence level: documented political crisis with disputed motives

Full story
----------
The 1965 Apostasia crisis broke public confidence in parliamentary democracy before the dictatorship. The fight over royal power, party discipline, defections, intelligence suspicion, and control of the military turned ordinary government formation into a legitimacy crisis.

Confirmed record
----------------
- The resignation of Georgios Papandreou and subsequent governments formed with defectors are documented political events.
- The palace played an active constitutional and political role.
- The crisis deepened polarization and distrust before the 1967 coup.

Disputed or unresolved points
-----------------------------
- Whether specific deputies acted from conviction, pressure, ambition, or external encouragement varies by person and source.
- The degree of foreign or intelligence involvement is debated.

Conspiracy theory / rumor lane
------------------------------
- The conspiracy lane claims a coordinated palace-foreign-intelligence operation to prevent center-left control; evidence supports pressure networks, but a single command chain is not fully proven.
- The weaker but useful theory is elite veto power: institutions behaved as if some electoral outcomes were unacceptable.

Source trail
------------
- Greek parliamentary record 1965
- Historical studies of palace politics
- Contemporaneous Greek press archives

Reader warning
--------------
This record includes conspiracy theories because they shape public memory. They are explicitly separated from documented anchors and should not be treated as proven unless the source trail supports them.
