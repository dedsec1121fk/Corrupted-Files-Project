Why this file matters:
Another recurring "conspiracy" theme is that governments expose populations to radiation or toxic risk, then spend decades minimizing, studying, litigating, and partially compensating the damage. The U.S. downwinders record gives that theme a real paper trail.

Core record:
- The Department of Justice states that the Radiation Exposure Compensation Act (RECA), enacted in 1990, provides compensation to certain people who contracted serious diseases after exposure to radiation from atmospheric nuclear testing or uranium industry work.
- The Department of Labor's RECA/EEOICPA brochure says eligible nuclear weapons workers and some exposed communities can receive compensation and medical benefits under federal law.
- NCI fallout material says the Nevada Test Site conducted more than 100 tests at or above ground level from 1951 through mid-1962, and radioactive debris was deposited to varying degrees over most of the continental U.S.
- EPA's fallout overview explains how radionuclides from aboveground tests mixed with debris and returned to Earth as fallout.
- National Academies material reviewing the CDC/NCI fallout work shows the federal government eventually had to estimate exposure to iodine-131 and related cancer risk from Nevada testing.

Why this belongs in the archive:
This is not a vague theory anymore. It is a documented case where the U.S. government later created compensation structures because exposure pathways and harms were real enough to require redress.

What is proved:
- Atmospheric testing generated fallout affecting U.S. populations.
- Congress and the executive branch created compensation frameworks because the issue was real, not imaginary.
- Health-risk estimation and compensation came after the exposures, not before them.

What remains open:
- Many victims and advocates argue compensation has always been too narrow or too late.
- The official compensation structure does not settle every historical argument about the full number of people harmed.
