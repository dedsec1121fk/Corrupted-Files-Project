Core story
----------
The CIA backed the overthrow of Guatemala’s elected government after land reform threatened
powerful interests and Cold War fears were invoked.

What happened, in full context
------------------------------
The coup against Jacobo Árbenz became a classic Cold War case: anti-communist language,
covert operations, propaganda, exile forces and corporate interests overlapped. United
Fruit’s land holdings and lobbying environment shaped Washington perceptions. The overthrow
contributed to decades of instability and violence in Guatemala.

Timeline
--------
- 1952: land reform begins.
- 1954: CIA-backed operation helps remove Árbenz.
- Aftermath: Guatemala enters long period of repression and conflict.

Key facts and evidence points
-----------------------------
- CIA role is documented in declassified histories.
- Land reform threatened United Fruit holdings.
- The coup destabilized Guatemala for decades.

Why this case matters
---------------------
It shows how foreign policy can mix ideology, business pressure and intelligence action.

Competing explanations and caution
-----------------------------------
Officials framed the operation as anti-communist containment. Critics see corporate
influence and anti-reform politics as central.
