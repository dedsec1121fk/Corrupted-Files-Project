Summary:
One reason JFK remains in conspiracy culture is that an official U.S. congressional committee did not simply reaffirm the Warren Commission in full.
The House Select Committee on Assassinations concluded that Lee Harvey Oswald fired the shots that struck President Kennedy, but it also concluded that scientific acoustical evidence established a high probability that two gunmen fired and that President Kennedy was probably assassinated as a result of a conspiracy.
At the same time, the committee said it could not identify the other gunman or the extent of the conspiracy, and it stated that the Soviet government, Cuban government, Secret Service, FBI, and CIA were not involved as institutions.

Why it matters:
- This is one of the most misunderstood official findings in U.S. history.
- It does NOT prove every JFK theory.
- It DOES show that an official federal committee concluded the case was probably larger than a lone-gunman-only story as then presented.

Key details from the archival summary:
- The committee found a high probability that two gunmen fired.
- It said Kennedy was probably assassinated as a result of a conspiracy.
- It said the investigation into the possibility of conspiracy had been inadequate.
- It said conclusions had been presented in a fashion that was too definitive.

Caution:
Later experts have disputed parts of the acoustics evidence.
This file records the official HSCA finding and why it matters historically; it does not treat every later theory as proven fact.
