Core story
----------
Federal troops killed Lakota people near Wounded Knee Creek after years of dispossession and
coercive policy.

What happened, in full context
------------------------------
The event is often described as a battle in older official language, but historical work and
Native testimony treat it as a massacre rooted in broken treaties, forced reservation life,
fear of the Ghost Dance movement, and military pressure. The file matters because it shows
how state narratives can rename violence and how the archive can preserve official language
while communities preserve trauma.

Timeline
--------
- 1890: Seventh Cavalry confronts Lakota near Wounded Knee.
- After a shot or struggle, soldiers fire heavily into the camp.
- Later decades: Native activists and historians challenge the official battle framing.

Key facts and evidence points
-----------------------------
- Hundreds of Lakota men, women and children died; exact numbers vary by source.
- The event followed US suppression of the Ghost Dance movement.
- Medals awarded to soldiers remain a major controversy.

Why this case matters
---------------------
Wounded Knee is a foundation case for understanding how law, military force, racial
ideology, and land policy can merge into a single system.

Competing explanations and caution
-----------------------------------
The basic killing is not in doubt. Interpretations differ over immediate triggers, but the
deeper context of coercive policy is central.

Source trail / suggested verification
-------------------------------------
