Summary:
Watergate began with the June 17, 1972 break-in at Democratic National Committee headquarters in the Watergate complex, but it expanded into a much wider scandal involving illegal surveillance, hush money, obstruction, abuse of executive power, and a constitutional crisis. National Archives material describes the affair as a national trauma and a test of the rule of law.

Why it matters:
Watergate is a key case where “it’s bigger than the break-in” turned out to be true. The scandal showed how intelligence-style tactics, covert political operations, and executive cover-ups can fuse into one system.

Timeline:
- May-June 1972: Bugging equipment is installed and burglars are arrested.
- 1972-1973: Public denials unravel and congressional investigations deepen.
- 1973-1974: The White House tapes become central evidence.
- August 1974: Richard Nixon resigns.
- The Watergate Special Prosecution Force continues handling related prosecutions and abuse-of-power issues.

What is proved:
- The DNC break-in occurred.
- The scandal involved more than the break-in itself.
- The Special Prosecutor’s records and the “Road Map” document the broader abuse-of-power case.
