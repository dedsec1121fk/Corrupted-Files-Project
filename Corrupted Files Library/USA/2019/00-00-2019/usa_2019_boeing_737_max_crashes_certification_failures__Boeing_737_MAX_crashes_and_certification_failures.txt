Country: USA

Core story
----------
Two deadly crashes exposed problems in Boeing’s 737 MAX design, training assumptions and FAA
certification oversight.

What happened, in full context
------------------------------
The 737 MAX scandal is a case about engineering culture under market pressure. A system
meant to prevent stalls became tied to fatal accidents, and regulators had delegated
significant oversight to the manufacturer. The human cost made visible a deeper issue:
safety systems can be weakened by schedule, competition and trust-based certification.

Timeline
--------
- 2018: Lion Air crash.
- 2019: Ethiopian Airlines crash and global grounding.
- Aftermath: redesign, settlements and oversight changes.

Key facts and evidence points
-----------------------------
- Crashes in 2018 and 2019 killed 346 people.
- MAX fleet was grounded globally.
- Boeing faced criminal and civil consequences.

Why this case matters
---------------------
It shows how captured or delegated regulation can fail in high-stakes technology.

Competing explanations and caution
-----------------------------------
Responsibility is shared across design, disclosure, training, certification and airline
operation; simplistic single-cause claims are weak.
