Full story
----------
Therac-25 radiation overdoses became a foundational software-safety case: patients were harmed by a medical device where software design, interface assumptions, weak incident reporting, and overconfidence in automation intersected.

Confirmed record
----------------
- Multiple patients received massive radiation overdoses in the 1980s.
- Investigations identified software and safety-process failures.
- The case is widely taught in safety-critical software engineering.

Disputed or unresolved points
-----------------------------
- Technical blame must be separated between code defects, interface behavior, testing, reporting, and regulation.
- The case is not proof that all medical automation is unsafe.

Conspiracy theory / rumor lane
------------------------------
- The theory lane says vendors hid software danger to protect profit; the documented lesson is that weak reporting and overconfidence delayed recognition.
- A structural reading is that invisible code can become an uninspected machine part inside the human body.
