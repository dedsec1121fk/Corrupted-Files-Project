MOVE bombing in Philadelphia
============================

Country: USA
Year folder: 1985
Category: policing / urban violence

Core story
----------
Philadelphia police dropped an explosive on a rowhouse occupied by MOVE members, causing
deaths and destroying homes.

What happened, in full context
------------------------------
The MOVE bombing is one of the most shocking domestic policing cases in modern US history.
The state treated a standoff as a militarized problem and used force that burned a
neighborhood. The dead included children. The case forces readers to confront how policing
can become collective punishment when a group is dehumanized as an enemy.

Timeline
--------
- 1985: police confrontation with MOVE escalates.
- Bomb dropped; fire spreads.
- Aftermath: commissions, lawsuits and public memory battles.

Key facts and evidence points
-----------------------------
- Eleven people died, including children.
- A city block burned.
- Philadelphia later apologized.

Why this case matters
---------------------
It belongs beside Waco, Ruby Ridge and other militarized domestic operations, but it
happened in a city neighborhood.

Competing explanations and caution
-----------------------------------
Officials framed it as a dangerous armed confrontation. The scale and method of response
remain the central outrage.
