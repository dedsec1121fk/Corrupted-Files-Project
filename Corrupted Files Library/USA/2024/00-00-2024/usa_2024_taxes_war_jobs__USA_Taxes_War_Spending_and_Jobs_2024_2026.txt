USA Taxes, War Spending, and Jobs: 2024-2026

Why this file exists:
A lot of people do not just ask about scandals.
They ask a simpler question:
Where does the money go, what does the state prioritize, and what kind of work future is being built?

This file is not a claim that “all tax money is stolen.”
It is a record of spending priorities, budget pressure, military allocation, and labor-market stress.

1) Taxes on labour are real, even if the USA is not the highest-tax OECD country.
The OECD says the tax wedge for the average single worker in the United States was 30.1% in 2024.
That was below the OECD average of 34.9%, but it still means a large share of labour cost is absorbed by taxes and social contributions.
Key link:
- https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/04/taxing-wages-2025-country-notes_16d47563/united-states_e44f6362/4a2a60b9-en.pdf

2) Huge public money flows now go to deficits and interest even before adding new promises.
The Congressional Budget Office said the federal budget deficit was $1.8 trillion in fiscal year 2025.
CBO also said net interest costs totaled $970 billion in 2025.
That means a very large amount of public money is being consumed just by debt service.
Key link:
- https://www.cbo.gov/publication/62286

3) The military side is still massive.
SIPRI says US military expenditure reached $997 billion in 2024, up 5.7% from 2023.
SIPRI also says the USA accounted for 37% of world military spending in 2024 and 66% of NATO spending.
Key link:
- https://www.sipri.org/sites/default/files/2025-04/2504_fs_milex_2024.pdf

4) NATO pressure points toward even more security allocation.
NATO says that at the 2025 Hague Summit, Allies committed to investing 5% of GDP annually by 2035:
at least 3.5% on core defence and up to 1.5% on defence- and security-related spending.
This is not a rumor.
It is an official future direction for alliance spending.
Key link:
- https://www.nato.int/en/what-we-do/introduction-to-nato/defence-expenditures-and-natos-5-commitment

5) Jobs are not collapsing in a movie-style instant, but the labor market is cooling and changing.
The OECD said in late 2025 that US job growth had cooled considerably,
the vacancy rate had returned to normal levels,
and hiring and quit rates were at their lowest levels in over a decade.
Key link:
- https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-economic-outlook-volume-2025-issue-2_9f653ca1-en/full-report/united-states_3d9917c1.html

6) AI pressure is already visible in early data.
The IMF said in January 2026 that employment levels in AI-vulnerable occupations were 3.6% lower after five years
in regions with high demand for AI skills than in regions with less demand.
The same IMF work said roughly 1 in 10 job postings in advanced economies now requires at least one new skill.
Key links:
- https://www.imf.org/en/blogs/articles/2026/01/14/new-skills-and-ai-are-reshaping-the-future-of-work
- https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/sdn/2026/english/sdnea2026001.pdf

7) The elite planning layer is open, not hidden.
The World Economic Forum says its Future of Jobs Report 2025 draws on over 1,000 employers
representing more than 14 million workers across 55 economies.
Whether someone likes the WEF or not, this shows that large institutions openly model and manage workforce transformation at scale.
Key link:
- https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/

The strongest “connect-the-dots” conclusion here is not “taxes are secretly disappearing into one black hole.”
It is that public money is under simultaneous pressure from debt service, defence commitments, and economic restructuring,
while workers face slower hiring, new skills pressure, and AI-linked polarization.
That creates the feeling that ordinary people are paying into systems they control less and less.

Extra context:
For the U.S., the strongest pattern is not simple taxation alone but the combination of persistent deficits, rising net-interest costs, very large military commitments, and labor-market uncertainty from automation and AI. Even without a secret plot, this mix can feel dystopian because everyday insecurity rises while strategic and defense spending stays politically durable.
