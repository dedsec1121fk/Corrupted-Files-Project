Summary:
The strongest version of the tobacco story is already bad enough without exaggeration:
- The U.S. Department of Justice sued major cigarette companies under RICO.
- In 2006, Judge Gladys Kessler found that the companies had violated civil racketeering law and had engaged in a decades-long scheme to deceive the public about smoking and health.
- The later corrective-statements regime forced the companies to publicly post and publish statements about health harms, addictiveness, manipulation of cigarettes, and misleading "light" or "low tar" claims.
- This is one of the clearest documented examples of a corporate sector systematically shaping science communication, public understanding, and product perception for profit over many decades.

Why it matters:
This case is important because it shows that "they knew and lied for years" is not always paranoia. In this case, a federal court found a coordinated deceptive pattern.

What this archive does NOT claim:
- It does NOT claim every smoking-related regulatory decision was fake.
- It does NOT claim smoking harms were hidden forever; many scientists, journalists, and public-health officials fought back.
- It does claim that the deception lasted long enough to become a generational public-health disaster.

Timeline add-on:
- 1950s onward: cigarette makers faced growing scientific evidence on cancer and other harms.
- 1999: DOJ filed the federal lawsuit.
- 2006: federal court found the companies liable under civil RICO for a long-running scheme to deceive the public.
- 2022 and after: corrective statements continued to be enforced in public-facing channels.

What is proved / what is not:
- PROVED: a federal court found a decades-long deceptive pattern.
- PROVED: the industry was ordered to carry corrective statements.
- NOT PROVED: every policy dispute around nicotine products today is part of one unified ongoing conspiracy.
