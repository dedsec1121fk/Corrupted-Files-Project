1) Toxic exposure, delayed accountability, and institutional denial
Status: [DOCUMENTED / STRONG EVIDENCE]

Summary:
Camp Lejeune became one of the most notorious U.S. toxic-exposure cases after years of contamination in drinking water systems used by Marines, families, and civilian workers. The scandal is not only about contamination itself, but also about evidence that warnings were missed, delayed, or inadequately disclosed.

Why it matters:
This case is a model of how bureaucratic institutions can expose people to serious harm for years and then fight, delay, or narrow accountability rather than act transparently.

What is proved:
- GAO has reported that contamination at Camp Lejeune lasted for decades and that the contamination period most commonly referenced ran through the 1950s-1980s.
- GAO said a Marine Corps-chartered panel criticized some actions that appeared designed to minimize or cover up information indicating contamination in Camp Lejeune drinking water.
- GAO cited the Congressional Budget Office estimate that about 650,000 people were stationed at Camp Lejeune at some point during the contamination period.
- Later GAO work and broader DOD toxic-exposure reporting show that military toxic-exposure and contamination liabilities remain a major long-tail issue, not an isolated historical footnote.

What this archive does NOT claim:
- It does NOT claim that every disease reported by former residents was caused by the water.
- It does NOT claim there was one single written order saying "hide everything." The record instead shows criticism of actions that minimized, delayed, or inadequately surfaced the danger.

Detailed timeline:
- 1950s-1980s: contaminated water systems serve Camp Lejeune personnel and families.
- 1980s: contamination becomes increasingly visible through testing and environmental review.
- 2007 and 2011: GAO publishes testimony and review material describing contamination, cleanup, and criticism of prior handling.
- 2020s: Congress, courts, and the Department of Defense continue dealing with health, claims, and toxic-exposure consequences.
- 2024: GAO's broader toxic-exposure report shows how large the military toxic-record universe remains, with millions of records and ongoing exposure tracking.
