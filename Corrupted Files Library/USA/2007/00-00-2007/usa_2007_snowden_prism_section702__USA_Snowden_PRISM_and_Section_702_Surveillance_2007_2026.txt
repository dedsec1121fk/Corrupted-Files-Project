USA: Snowden, PRISM, and Section 702 Surveillance (2007-2026)

1) Content collection, incidental collection, and the normalization of mass-scale communications surveillance
Status: [DOCUMENTED]

Summary:
Section 702 of FISA allows surveillance targeting of non-U.S. persons reasonably believed to be outside the United States, with compelled assistance from electronic communication providers. The modern public debate exploded after the Snowden leaks, which exposed PRISM and related collection systems to worldwide attention. PCLOB’s 2014 and 2023 reports, plus its 2026 follow-up and ODNI transparency reporting, confirm that the program is extensive, highly technical, and subject to recurring compliance and civil-liberties debates.

Why it matters:
This file is important because it moves the archive from older Cold War abuses into contemporary digital surveillance. The reality is not “they read every message from every person manually.” It is a system where large-scale access, incidental collection, U.S.-person queries, and secret court-approved frameworks coexist.

What is proved:
- Section 702 is real and collects communications content under its legal framework.
- PRISM was a real collection program discussed in declassified material and PCLOB reporting.
- ODNI continues publishing annual statistical transparency reports showing the scale and oversight structure of 702.

What remains contested:
- Officials argue the program is indispensable for national security.
- Critics argue the framework still permits overly broad collection, mission creep, and insufficient protection for Americans whose communications are swept in.
