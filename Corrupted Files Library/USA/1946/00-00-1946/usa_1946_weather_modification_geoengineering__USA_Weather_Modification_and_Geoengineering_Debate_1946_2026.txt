1) Real atmospheric intervention history versus exaggerated claims
Status: [DOCUMENTED / PARTLY PROVED]

Summary:
The U.S. record shows that weather modification is real, but limited. Cloud seeding, hurricane-modification experiments, and modern geoengineering debates all exist in the documentary record. What is not proved is full-spectrum "weather control" over storms, earthquakes, or global climate at will.

Why it matters:
Many people are told that any concern about weather manipulation is insane. That is wrong. The truthful position is narrower: atmospheric intervention has been researched and sometimes practiced, but its actual capabilities are much smaller than maximal conspiracy claims suggest.

What is proved:
- NOAA maintains a library of weather-modification project reports.
- NOAA's hurricane-history materials describe Project STORMFURY, an experimental hurricane-modification program active from 1962 to 1983.
- NOAA says cloud seeding is the only common weather-modification activity currently practiced in the United States, typically by private companies or state/local projects, and explicitly says NOAA itself does not fund or participate in those projects.
- NOAA's 2024 fact-check says cloud seeding is real but rejects exaggerated online claims about federal agencies controlling major weather events.

What this archive does NOT claim:
- It does NOT claim the U.S. can steer hurricanes at will.
- It does NOT claim every extreme weather event is artificial.
- It does NOT treat geoengineering discussion as proof that secret global deployment has already happened.

Detailed timeline:
- 1946 onward: cloud-seeding era begins in U.S. weather-modification history.
- 1962-1983: Project STORMFURY explores hurricane modification by seeding.
- 1980s-present: cloud seeding continues in narrower water-resource and hail contexts.
- 2024: NOAA publicly debunks exaggerated claims while acknowledging the reality of cloud seeding and limited weather-modification activity.
- 2026: NOAA's report archive continues to preserve the institutional record of weather-modification projects.
