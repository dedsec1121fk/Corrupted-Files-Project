Country: Greece
Year: 1996

Evidence level: documented crisis with disputed diplomatic handling

Full story
----------
The Imia/Kardak crisis brought Greece and Turkey close to war over small islets. The public-trust issue is how symbols, television pressure, military posture, alliance diplomacy, and secrecy can compress decision-making until citizens cannot tell what was prevented and what was conceded.

Confirmed record
----------------
- The crisis occurred in January 1996 and involved Greek and Turkish forces.
- Three Greek officers died in a helicopter crash during the crisis.
- U.S. mediation helped de-escalate the confrontation.

Disputed or unresolved points
-----------------------------
- The exact diplomatic wording, concessions, and military interpretation remain contested in Greek public debate.
- Conspiracy claims about the helicopter crash require evidence beyond suspicion.

Conspiracy theory / rumor lane
------------------------------
- Some theories claim a forced humiliation arranged by allies; mediation pressure is documented, but claims of scripted surrender need stronger proof.
- A safer structural theory is escalation by media spectacle and weak crisis communication.

Source trail
------------

- Greek and Turkish public record on Imia/Kardak
