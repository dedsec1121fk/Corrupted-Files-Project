1. The story
Three years after the Tempi train disaster, Greece still carried the crash as an open wound. The
deaths of 57 people, many of them young, became a national accusation: not only against one
stationmaster or one night, but against decades of delayed safety systems, neglected
infrastructure, weak oversight, political responsibility, and a public sector where warnings too
often become memorials.

2. Background
Tempi is already in the database, but the third-year moment deserves its own file because
disasters change over time. First there is shock. Then there are funerals. Then technical
reports. Then court files. Then political battles over evidence, responsibility, and memory. A
society often learns more from the third year than from the first week, because that is when the
system reveals whether it can hold itself accountable.

3. Chronology and turning points
- 28 February 2023: passenger and freight trains collided near Tempi; 57 people died.
- 2023–2024: protests, investigations, and public anger focused on rail safety and political
responsibility.
- 2025: inquiry findings and recommendations highlighted safety gaps and systemic failures.
- 2026: anniversary demonstrations and the approach of trial proceedings kept the case central in
Greek public life.
- Families continued demanding accountability, transparency, and preservation of memory.

4. Psychological / social-control angle
The psychological power of Tempi comes from youth and preventability. People can accept that
accidents happen; they cannot accept that warnings were ignored, systems delayed, and
responsibility diluted. The phrase “πάρε με όταν φτάσεις” became a social wound because it turns
ordinary family care into a national indictment.

5. What is documented

6. What remains disputed, exaggerated, or unresolved
