1) The triangle of political power, business power, and weak enforcement
Status: [STRONG EVIDENCE]

Summary:
The austerity-era Greek crisis was not only a debt story. It was also a story about who could break rules, influence the narrative, avoid taxes, receive state favor, and survive crisis conditions while ordinary people paid the price. Reuters reporting from the period repeatedly described an environment shaped by tax evasion, political patrons, banking weakness, and powerful business-media actors.

Why it matters:
This file is here because many people experience corruption not only as one big scandal but as a system: selective enforcement, connected debtors, protected media owners, weak collection from the powerful, and much harsher pressure on everyone else.

What is proved / strongly supported:
- Reuters' 2010 special report said Greece had promised the EU and IMF to go after tax dodgers and end what the IMF had called wide tax evasion and corruption.
- Reuters' 2011 report described continuing failures in Greece's anti-evasion drive despite public promises.
- Reuters' 2015 "war on oligarchs" report described investigators saying that cases of oil smuggling, banking fraud, and tax evasion involved wealthy businessmen with political patrons.
- The overall picture was one of a political economy where media ownership, contracting, credit, and political leverage overlapped heavily.

What this archive does NOT claim:
- It does NOT claim every wealthy Greek businessman committed crimes.
- It does NOT claim the state never pursued elite cases. The problem documented here is the pattern of weak, selective, or delayed enforcement.

Detailed timeline:
- 2010: under bailout pressure, Greece publicly vows to crack down on tax evasion and corruption.
- 2011: Reuters reports the anti-evasion effort still looks like a cat-and-mouse game, with limited concrete results.
- 2013-2015: major banking and corruption scandals continue feeding the public perception that connected actors are treated differently.
- 2015: Reuters reports Tsipras's government frames a political battle against oligarchs and patronage networks.
