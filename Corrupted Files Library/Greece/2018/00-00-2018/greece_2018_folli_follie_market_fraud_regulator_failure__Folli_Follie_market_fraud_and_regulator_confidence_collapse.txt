Full story
----------
The Folli Follie case damaged confidence in Greek market oversight because a listed company with global branding was accused of inflating sales, cash, and Asian operations. The scandal asked a simple question: how long can a market believe a story when auditors, regulators, investors, and banks all depend on documents they may not truly verify?

Confirmed record
----------------
- The scandal became public in 2018 after investor criticism and market scrutiny.
- Greek authorities pursued criminal allegations around financial statements and investor deception.
- The case exposed weak verification in a market that needed credibility after years of crisis.

Disputed or unresolved points
-----------------------------
- The legal process decides individual guilt and responsibility.
- The broader regulatory question is whether the system missed red flags or lacked tools to act quickly.

Conspiracy theory / rumor lane
------------------------------
- Some market rumors alleged powerful protection because the company was too connected to fail; this is an allegation unless tied to documents.
- A better supported theory is incentives: everyone in the chain preferred growth narratives until evidence forced collapse.

Why it matters

It matters because corporate fraud is also a public-trust case when ordinary investors assume audited numbers are real.
