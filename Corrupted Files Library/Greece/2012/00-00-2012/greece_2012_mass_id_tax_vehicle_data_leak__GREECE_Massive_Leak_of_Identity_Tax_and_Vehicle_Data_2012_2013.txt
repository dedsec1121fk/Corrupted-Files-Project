Summary:
One of the most disturbing Greek data cases involved the apparent exposure of a huge share of the population's records:
- A programmer was reported to have possessed millions of files containing identity-card data, addresses, tax identification numbers, and licence-plate data.
- Much of the data may have been duplicated, but the scale was still alarming enough to trigger national attention.
- Greece's data-protection authority later fined the finance ministry over failures connected to a major tax-data leak.

Why it matters:
This case matters because it shows how fragmented state databases do not automatically equal real security. Data can still be assembled, copied, sold, or abused at population scale.

What this archive does NOT claim:
- It does NOT claim the leak proved a single central master database.
- It does NOT claim every file represented a unique citizen.
- It does claim that the scale was serious enough to raise questions about state data protection and insider-access risk.
