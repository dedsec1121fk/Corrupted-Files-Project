1. The story
In recent years, Greece has faced repeated public outrage over femicides and domestic violence
cases, especially where victims had contacted authorities or were known to be in danger. These
cases are not one scandal but a pattern file: the system often asks victims to report danger,
then fails to act with the urgency that danger requires.

2. Background
Domestic violence is a private terror with public consequences. Police stations, prosecutors,
social services, shelters, emergency lines, and courts form the protection chain. When one link
treats a threat as a domestic argument, the chain breaks. Greece has introduced reforms and
hotlines, but public anger remains strong because high-profile cases keep showing gaps between
policy on paper and safety in real life.

3. Chronology and turning points
- 2020s: public discussion of femicide intensified in Greece.
- Several high-profile killings led to criticism of police response, protective measures, and risk
assessment.
- Authorities announced or expanded domestic-violence offices, reporting tools, and training
efforts.
- Civil-society groups continued to argue that victims need faster protection, shelters,
electronic monitoring options, and serious accountability for failures.

4. Psychological / social-control angle
The psychological control here is intimate before it is institutional: abusers isolate,
threaten, monitor, and exhaust victims. When the state responds slowly, the victim receives the
message that escape is risky and help is uncertain. Public trust collapses when citizens see
that a police report may become a final trace instead of a shield.
