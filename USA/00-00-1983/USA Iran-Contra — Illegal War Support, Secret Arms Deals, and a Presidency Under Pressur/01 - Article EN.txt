1) A scandal that joined two operations that were both supposed to be constrained

Summary:
Iran-Contra remains one of the clearest modern U.S. examples of covert policy being run around Congress and then collapsing into public scandal.
The core structure was this:
- members of the Reagan administration supported the Contra rebels in Nicaragua even after Congress passed restrictions under the Boland framework;
- officials also arranged secret or illicit arms sales to Iran;
- when Reagan later acknowledged that the two operations were connected, the scandal escalated sharply.

Why it matters:
- It is a major proof-of-record case that the U.S. national security state can run hidden foreign-policy programs that bypass the spirit or letter of democratic oversight.
- It shows how “anti-communism,” “national security,” and secrecy can be used to justify operations that would not survive open debate.

What strong archival sources say:
The National Security Archive describes Iran-Contra as the biggest scandal since Watergate. It says the affair centered on illegal backing of the Contra rebels and illicit sales of high-tech weapons to Iran.
The National Archives keeps the records of Independent Counsel Lawrence Walsh, whose investigation ran from 1986 to 1993 and generated thousands of pages of records, including classified and sensitive material later released.

Key record points:
- public scandal broke in late 1986;
- covert support for the Contras became a constitutional crisis because Congress had tried to restrict that support;
- secret arms transactions with Iran were politically explosive on their own;
- the scandal deepened once the linkage between the two operations became public;
- Walsh's records show how large, legally complicated, and long-running the aftermath became.

What this does NOT prove:
- It does not prove every later theory about every covert U.S. action.
- It does prove that covert policy, deception, and executive-branch workarounds of oversight were real and documented in a major case.

Useful reading logic:
If someone says “governments would never secretly support a war or secretly trade with an enemy while publicly saying something else,” Iran-Contra is one of the strongest counterexamples in the modern record.
