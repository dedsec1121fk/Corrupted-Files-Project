Triangle Shirtwaist fire, locked exits, and labor-safety reform

Country: USA
Year: 1911
Category: labor safety / industrial disaster / regulatory reform
Evidence level: documented industrial disaster and reform catalyst

Full story
----------
The Triangle Shirtwaist fire killed garment workers in New York and exposed locked exits, weak safety rules, immigrant labor exploitation, and the cost of treating workplace risk as a private business matter.

Confirmed record
----------------
- 146 workers died in the 1911 fire.
- The disaster became a major catalyst for workplace-safety reform.
- The victims were largely immigrant women and girls.

Disputed or unresolved points
-----------------------------
- The owners' legal liability and moral responsibility were not treated the same by the public.
- Reform memory can obscure the years of ignored worker warnings before the disaster.

Conspiracy theory / rumor lane
------------------------------
- The theory lane says deaths were the predictable result of a system that valued production over immigrant lives; that is a structural claim, not a hidden-plot claim.
- A stronger reading is regulatory absence: profit shaped the safety floor until bodies forced law to catch up.

Reader warning
--------------
The theory lane is included because rumors and conspiracy claims shape public memory. It is not an endorsement; the reader should privilege primary records, official findings, court files, and named investigations.
