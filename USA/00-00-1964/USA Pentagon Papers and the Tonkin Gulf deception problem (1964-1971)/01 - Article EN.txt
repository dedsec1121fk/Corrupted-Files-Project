Summary:
The Pentagon Papers are one of the strongest documentary records of official deception in modern U.S. history. National Archives says the Papers were the Defense Department's secret Vietnam study commissioned in 1967 and leaked in 1971. The Miller Center's summary of the archive says a theme that emerges is government deception, including that the Johnson administration deceived Congress in August 1964 as it sought the Tonkin Gulf Resolution.

Why it matters:
- It is a core example of how war narratives can be shaped before the public sees the real internal record.
- It shows the gap between internal planning and external messaging.
- It remains one of the most important “connect the dots” cases because the dots are documented.

Key points:

- The Tonkin episode became a case study in how disputed or misleading information can help unlock major escalation.
