Tuskegee syphilis study, medical racism, and informed-consent betrayal — PROOF DOSSIER

Verified anchors
----------------
- The U.S. Public Health Service ran the study from 1932 until public exposure in 1972.
- Participants were misled and did not receive treatment in the way modern ethics would require.
- The case led to major reforms in human-subject research ethics.

Disputed / weaker evidence
--------------------------
- The precise health outcomes for each participant and family vary across records.
- Public memory sometimes compresses the case into claims that need careful wording.

Conspiracy theories to handle carefully
---------------------------------------
- A common theory says the government injected men with syphilis; the documented core is different: treatment was withheld and consent was corrupt.
- The broader distrust of medical systems is understandable, but each later claim about hidden experiments needs separate proof.

Source trail
------------
- CDC historical materials on Tuskegee
- Belmont Report and research ethics record
- U.S. presidential apology and settlement history

Evidence discipline: court findings, official reports, regulatory records, parliamentary files, and named investigative reporting are stronger than rumor, anonymous posts, or claims that require a hidden master plan.
