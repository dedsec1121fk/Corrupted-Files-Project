1. The story
In 1985, Philadelphia police dropped an explosive on a house occupied by the Black liberation
group MOVE. A fire followed, eleven people died, including children, and a city block burned.
The case is one of the most extreme examples of American police power becoming military force
inside a residential neighborhood.

2. Background
MOVE had a long conflict with city authorities. The group’s behavior, neighbours’ complaints,
prior confrontations, and police hostility created a crisis atmosphere. But the central question
is proportionality: how did a municipal police operation become an aerial bombing? The event
forced the public to confront how quickly “public order” language can hide a decision that
endangers an entire community.

3. Chronology and turning points
- Before 1985: MOVE and Philadelphia authorities had years of conflict.
- May 13, 1985: police attempted to remove MOVE members from a row house.
- An explosive device was dropped onto the building.
- A fire spread and authorities allowed it to burn for a period; eleven people died and dozens of
homes were destroyed.
- Investigations and public memory continued to treat the event as a symbol of racialized state
violence.

4. Psychological / social-control angle
The MOVE bombing created a civic trauma: people saw the state use tactics associated with war
and then speak in bureaucratic language afterward. For communities already distrustful of
policing, the message was terrifying: the government could turn a neighbourhood into a
battlefield and later call it a mistake.

5. What is documented
- Police dropped an explosive device.
- The fire killed adults and children.
- Homes in the surrounding neighborhood were destroyed.
- Official investigations criticized decisions made by authorities.

6. What remains disputed, exaggerated, or unresolved
- MOVE’s own actions and conflicts with neighbors remain part of the story; acknowledging that
does not justify the bombing.
- Debates continue over which officials bore the greatest responsibility, but the disastrous state
action is not in doubt.

7. Why this belongs in Corrupted Files
The MOVE bombing belongs here because it shows corruption not as a secret bribe but as a
collapse of restraint. When agencies treat citizens as enemies and neighborhoods as combat
zones, law becomes force wearing a uniform.
