- In 1985 Philadelphia police dropped an explosive on a house occupied by MOVE members.
- The resulting fire killed adults and children and destroyed dozens of homes.
- The central conflict is how a city converted a policing operation into a neighborhood catastrophe.
- It matters because state force, racial politics, and urban governance collided in one visible disaster.
- Public trust collapsed because the event looked less like law enforcement and more like war against residents.
- Questions remain over command decisions, fire response, and accountability.
