Japanese American internment, wartime racism, and constitutional failure Country: USA Year: 1942 Category: civil liberties / wartime detention / racism Evidence level: documented mass detention with later government apology Full story ---------- After Pearl Harbor, the U.S. government forced Japanese Americans from the West Coast into camps. The case shows how fear, racism, military claims, and courts can combine to strip citizens and residents of liberty without individualized proof. Confirmed record ---------------- - Executive Order 9066 enabled mass exclusion and detention. - More than 100,000 people of Japanese ancestry were affected, many of them U.S. citizens. - The U.S. later apologized and provided redress through the Civil Liberties Act of 1988.
