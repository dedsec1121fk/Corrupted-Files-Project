Iran coup, CIA oil politics, democracy loss, and blowback

Country: USA
Year: 1953
Category: foreign intervention / oil / covert action
Evidence level: documented covert action with declassified record

Full story
----------
The 1953 overthrow of Mohammad Mosaddegh is a U.S. public-trust case because declassified records later showed covert action around oil nationalization, Cold War fear, and the removal of an elected government.

Confirmed record
----------------
- U.S. and British covert roles are documented in declassified and historical material.
- Oil nationalization and Cold War anti-communism shaped the intervention.
- The coup helped restore the Shah's power and influenced later Iranian distrust of the West.

Disputed or unresolved points
-----------------------------
- Iranian internal politics also mattered and should not be erased by a CIA-only story.
- The long-term chain from 1953 to 1979 is debated in degree, not relevance.

Conspiracy theory / rumor lane
------------------------------
- The conspiracy lane often treats oil companies as the only actor; the record is stronger when it connects oil, empire, Cold War policy, and Iranian factions.
- A structural reading is that covert action can win a short crisis and create decades of distrust.

Source trail
------------

- British-Iranian oil nationalization history
- Academic histories of the 1953 coup

Reader warning
--------------
