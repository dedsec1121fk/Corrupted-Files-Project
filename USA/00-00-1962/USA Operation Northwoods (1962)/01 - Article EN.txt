USA: Operation Northwoods (1962)

1) A declassified proposal for false-flag pretexts against Cuba
Status: [DOCUMENTED]
Summary:
Operation Northwoods is one of the clearest examples of a “that sounds too insane to be real” story that turned out to be based on actual declassified documents.
In 1962, the U.S. Joint Chiefs of Staff produced proposals for pretexts that could justify U.S. military intervention in Cuba. The now-public documents included ideas for staged incidents and manipulated narratives designed to create support for war.
What matters most is not that the plan happened — it did not. What matters is that top military leadership put such ideas on paper and sent them up the chain.

Why it matters:
- It shows that false-pretext thinking was not just a paranoid fantasy.
- It shows that internal state planning can include proposals that would be publicly unthinkable if exposed in real time.
- It is a strong reminder that “rejected” and “never implemented” still does not mean “never seriously proposed.”

What this archive does NOT claim:
- This file does NOT claim Operation Northwoods was carried out.
- This file does NOT claim every later national-security incident was a false flag.
- It claims only what the record supports: a real top-secret proposal existed, it was drafted inside the U.S. national-security system, and it contemplated manufactured pretexts for war.

Key details:
- The declassified document “Justification for U.S. Military Intervention in Cuba” was presented by the Joint Chiefs of Staff in March 1962.
- The National Security Archive summarized the plan as Pentagon proposals for pretexts to justify a Cuba invasion.
- Publicly available summaries of the declassified material describe plans involving staged incidents and blame assignment to Cuba.
- The plan was not implemented.
