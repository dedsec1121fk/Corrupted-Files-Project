2008 financial crisis, bailouts, and moral hazard

Country: USA
Year: 2008
Category: financial crisis / regulatory failure
Evidence level: documented crisis with contested blame and accountability

Full story
----------
The 2008 crisis exposed mortgage fraud, securitization risk, rating-agency failure, bank leverage, and regulatory blindness. The bailout debate became a public-trust wound because many institutions were saved while millions of households lost homes, jobs, savings, or future wealth.

Confirmed record
----------------
- The crisis led to emergency interventions including TARP, Federal Reserve facilities, and major bank rescues or failures.
- Official investigations documented failures in regulation, risk management, mortgage lending, and credit ratings.
- The public record shows few senior executives faced criminal punishment compared with the scale of harm.

Disputed or unresolved points
-----------------------------
- Debate continues over whether bailouts prevented depression or rewarded reckless institutions.
- The line between legal risk-taking and fraud varies across firms, products, and documents.

Conspiracy theory / rumor lane
------------------------------
- Some theories claim a planned wealth transfer from taxpayers to Wall Street; the bailout mechanics did transfer risk publicly, but proof of one coordinated secret plan is weaker.
- The better-supported structural claim is regulatory capture: rules, lobbying, complexity, and revolving doors favored finance over households.

Why it matters
--------------
It matters because trust breaks when losses are private for citizens but survivable for institutions with political access.

Source trail

Reader warning
--------------
This record deliberately separates documented facts from allegations and conspiracy theories. The theory lane is included because those claims shape public memory, not because every claim is proven.
