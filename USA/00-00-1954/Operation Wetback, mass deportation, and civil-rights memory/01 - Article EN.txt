Operation Wetback, mass deportation, and civil-rights memory

Country: USA
Year: 1954
Category: immigration enforcement / civil rights / labor
Evidence level: documented federal deportation campaign with contested numbers and abuses

Full story
----------
Operation Wetback was a 1954 U.S. immigration-enforcement campaign targeting Mexican migrants. The case belongs in the database because deportation power, agricultural labor demand, racial profiling, and political theater collided in ways that still shape border memory.

Confirmed record
----------------
- The federal campaign is documented in immigration history.
- Reported deportation numbers are disputed and often inflated in public memory.
- The operation occurred alongside labor systems that depended on Mexican workers.

Disputed or unresolved points
-----------------------------
- Exact numbers, voluntary departures, and forced removals must be separated.
- Abuse claims vary by location and record completeness.

Conspiracy theory / rumor lane
------------------------------
- The conspiracy lane says the campaign was designed to terrorize labor while preserving employer control; the labor-control pattern is plausible, but intent varies by actor.
- A structural reading is that border enforcement often punishes workers while sparing the demand system.

Reader warning
--------------
The theory lane is included because rumors and conspiracy claims shape public memory. It is not an endorsement; the reader should privilege primary records, official findings, court files, and named investigations.
