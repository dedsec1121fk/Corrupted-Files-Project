## English — Summary

The 1886 Haymarket affair began as a labor rally in Chicago, turned deadly after a bomb and police gunfire, and led to convictions of anarchists in a trial later condemned for weak evidence and political prejudice.

## English — Full Story

- Background — The eight-hour-day movement mobilized workers amid severe industrial conflict, anti-immigrant sentiment and fear of anarchism. A police killing at the McCormick plant intensified anger before the Haymarket meeting.

- Central episode — On 4 May 1886, as police moved to disperse a largely peaceful meeting, an unknown person threw a bomb. The explosion and subsequent gunfire killed police officers and civilians.

- Immediate response — Authorities arrested anarchist organizers and publishers. Eight men were convicted despite the prosecution's inability to prove that most had participated in the bombing; four were executed and one died in custody.

- Human and institutional impact — The affair damaged the labor movement in the short term, intensified repression of radicals and immigrants and created international martyrs associated with May Day.

- Accountability — Separate questions concern the unidentified bomber, police use of force, evidentiary standards at trial, press prejudice, jury selection and the later gubernatorial pardon.

- Legacy — Haymarket became a global symbol of labor rights and political injustice, while the identity of the bomber remains unresolved.
