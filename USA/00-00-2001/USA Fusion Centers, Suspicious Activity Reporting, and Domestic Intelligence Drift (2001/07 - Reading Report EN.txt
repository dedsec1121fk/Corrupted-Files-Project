Summary:
After 9/11, the United States built a large information-sharing architecture:
- Fusion centers were designed to connect federal, state, local, and sometimes private-sector threat information.
- Suspicious Activity Reporting systems were built to feed observations into intelligence-style pipelines.
- Oversight bodies later warned about weak measures of effectiveness, duplication, poor definitions of success, and civil-liberties concerns.
- The strongest documented critique is not that fusion centers stopped functioning entirely, but that the system expanded faster than proof of value.

Why it matters:
This is one of the clearest examples of bureaucracy growing under the banner of homeland security while oversight struggles to prove necessity, proportionality, and results.

What this archive does NOT claim:
- It does NOT claim every fusion-center analyst acts in bad faith.
- It does NOT claim there were zero useful outputs.
- It does claim that official oversight repeatedly found mission creep, overlap, and weak accountability.
