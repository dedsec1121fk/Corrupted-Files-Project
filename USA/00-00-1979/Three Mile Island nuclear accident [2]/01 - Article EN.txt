Three Mile Island nuclear accident
==================================

Country: USA
Year folder: 1979
Category: industrial risk / regulation
Evidence level: documented accident

Core story
----------
A partial nuclear meltdown in Pennsylvania revealed how technical failures, human error and
confused communication can magnify public fear.

What happened, in full context
------------------------------
Three Mile Island did not produce the mass casualty scenario feared by some, but it
permanently changed nuclear politics. The crisis was also informational: officials,
operators, journalists and citizens struggled to understand what was happening in real time.
It shows how complex infrastructure requires transparent communication as much as
engineering.

Timeline
--------
- 1979: accident unfolds.
- Afterward: cleanup and investigations.
- Long-term: nuclear expansion faces stronger resistance.

Key facts and evidence points
-----------------------------
- Partial meltdown occurred at Unit 2.
- Public communication failures increased fear.
- Nuclear regulation and industry practices changed.

Why this case matters
---------------------
It is a case study in low-probability/high-consequence systems and public trust.

Competing explanations and caution
-----------------------------------
Radiation-health impact claims vary; the most careful reading separates proven accident
facts from contested long-term health interpretations.

Source trail / suggested verification
-------------------------------------
- US Government Accountability Office: https://www.gao.gov/
