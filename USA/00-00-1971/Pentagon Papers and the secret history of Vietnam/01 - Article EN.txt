Pentagon Papers and the secret history of Vietnam
=================================================

Country: USA
Year folder: 1971
Category: secrecy / press freedom
Evidence level: documented leak and court battle

Core story
----------
A classified study showed US leaders had misled the public about Vietnam; its publication
triggered a landmark press-freedom fight.

What happened, in full context
------------------------------
The Pentagon Papers revealed not a single lie but a pattern of managed public narrative
across administrations. The state argued publication endangered security. The press argued
citizens could not judge war without truth. The Supreme Court case became a defining moment
for prior restraint and the role of newspapers against government secrecy.

Timeline
--------
- 1971: papers published.
- 1971: Supreme Court rules in major press case.
- Aftermath: distrust of official war narratives deepens.

Key facts and evidence points
-----------------------------
- Daniel Ellsberg leaked the study.
- New York Times and Washington Post faced legal pressure.
- The Supreme Court allowed publication to continue.

Why this case matters
---------------------
It demonstrates how democratic consent collapses when war information is hidden or
manipulated.

Competing explanations and caution
-----------------------------------
Some details were genuinely sensitive, but the public-interest value was enormous.
