COINTELPRO files, FBI disruption, and domestic politics

Country: USA
Year: 1971
Category: domestic surveillance / political disruption
Evidence level: documented FBI programs exposed by files and congressional investigation

Full story
----------
COINTELPRO moved beyond surveillance into disruption of civil-rights, antiwar, Black liberation, socialist, and other political groups. The public-trust issue is that state security power was used not only to observe possible crimes but to shape political life.

Confirmed record
----------------
- The 1971 burglary of an FBI office helped expose files.
- The Church Committee later documented FBI domestic abuses.
- Targets included Martin Luther King Jr., Black activists, antiwar groups, and others.

Disputed or unresolved points
-----------------------------
- The degree of disruption varied by target and field office.
- Some later claims about every protest being controlled by FBI exceed the record.

Conspiracy theory / rumor lane
------------------------------
- Many things once dismissed as paranoia were documented; that does not make every modern infiltration claim true.
- The structural theory is that secret policing drifts from security into political management when oversight is weak.

Source trail
------------
- Church Committee reports
- FBI COINTELPRO file releases
- National Security Archive and civil-rights records

Reader warning
--------------
This record includes conspiracy theories because they shape public memory. They are explicitly separated from documented anchors and should not be treated as proven unless the source trail supports them.
