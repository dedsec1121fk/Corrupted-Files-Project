Background
----------
Activists broke into an FBI office in Media, Pennsylvania, in 1971 and revealed files that helped expose COINTELPRO.

Chronology and public record
----------------------------
The files showed domestic surveillance and disruption against political groups, civil-rights movements, and dissent networks.

Main conflict
-------------
The key conflict is whether security agencies can secretly police lawful political activity without destroying democracy.

Why it matters
--------------
The case matters because it transformed abstract fears of surveillance into documents people could read.

Impact on public trust
----------------------
It produced long-term distrust of intelligence power and helped push later oversight reforms.

Open questions
--------------
Open questions remain about how much similar activity continues under different legal names.
