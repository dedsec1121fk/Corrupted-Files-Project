USA: Open-air biological tests on unwarned populations (1949-1969)

1) The Army sprayed simulants in cities and other locations without informed public consent
Status: [DOCUMENTED]

Summary:
A disturbing but well-documented part of U.S. Cold War history is the open-air testing of biological warfare simulants. GAO testimony in 1994 said that between 1949 and 1969 the Army conducted several hundred biological-warfare tests in which unaware populations were sprayed with bacterial tracers or simulants thought at the time to be harmless. Some tests covered large urban areas including San Francisco and St. Louis.

Why it matters:
- This is exactly the kind of thing many people would call too dystopian to be real.
- It shows that “for national security” officials were willing to treat whole populations as test environments.
- It also shows the danger of secrecy combined with official confidence that risks were low.

Key points:
- GAO says the Army conducted several hundred tests between 1949 and 1969.
- The 1994 GAO testimony explicitly mentions San Francisco, St. Louis, and the New York City subway system.
- Later GAO work on DOD human testing and chemical/biological defense shows how large the testing universe was and how uneven the record can be.

Sources:
- https://www.gao.gov/assets/t-nsiad-94-266.pdf
- https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-04-410.pdf
