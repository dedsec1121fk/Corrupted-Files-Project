USA: Teapot Dome and the Oil-Lease Corruption Scandal (1921-1929) 1) Secret leases, bribes, and the classic template for modern corruption scandals Status: [DOCUMENTED] Summary: Teapot Dome is one of the clearest early U.S. examples of what people now describe as a “deep-state style” corruption scandal, though in plain terms it was a cabinet-level corruption and cover-up case. Interior Secretary Albert B. Fall secretly leased U.S. naval petroleum reserves at Teapot Dome, Wyoming, and Elk Hills, California, to private oil companies without competitive bidding. Senate investigators later exposed the arrangement and uncovered bribery and “sweetheart” dealing tied to the leases. Why it matters: Teapot Dome matters because it shows that high-level corruption, hidden arrangements with private industry, and state capture are not new.
