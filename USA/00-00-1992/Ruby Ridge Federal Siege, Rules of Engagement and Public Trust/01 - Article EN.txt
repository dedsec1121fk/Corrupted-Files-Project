Ruby Ridge: Federal Siege, Rules of Engagement and Public Trust
===============================================================
Country: United States
Main year folder: 1992
Category: federal force / siege / civil liberties
Evidence level: documented deadly operation with official reviews
File role: Original dossier-style article for the Corrupted Files database. This is written from scratch so it can be redistributed with the archive.

1. Why this file exists
Ruby Ridge remains one of the cases that shaped modern distrust of federal law enforcement, especially around militarized tactics and rules of engagement.

2. Background and setting
The 1992 siege at the Weaver family property in Idaho led to deaths and later investigations into how federal agents planned, communicated and used force. The case became a symbolic reference point for anti-government movements and for civil-liberties critiques of federal power.

3. Chronology
- 1992: standoff begins after prior weapons case and failure to appear.
- August 1992: U.S. Marshal William Degan, Sammy Weaver and Vicki Weaver die in related events.
- Afterward: reviews, settlement and public hearings examine federal conduct.

4. What happened in practical terms
The mechanism was escalation: a criminal case became a siege, tactical assumptions hardened, and rules of engagement were criticized as too permissive for deadly force.

5. Evidence map
Documented elements:
- Deaths occurred during the operation.
- Official reviews criticized aspects of the rules and conduct.
- A settlement was reached with the family.

Alleged or disputed elements:
- Different accounts exist about threat perception during key moments.
- The wider ideological meaning of the case remains contested.

Unsupported or overextended claims to treat carefully:
- Claims that Ruby Ridge proves all federal law enforcement is illegitimate are political conclusions, not evidence.

6. Human impact
The immediate human cost was death and trauma; the broader cost was a new mythos of federal overreach that influenced later events.

7. Why the case created suspicion
Suspicion persisted because the government investigated its own use of force after lives were already lost.

8. Counterpoints and caution
The case should not be used to sanitize extremist ideology, but it must be studied as a real example of force escalation and accountability failure.

9. What a reader should conclude
Ruby Ridge shows how law enforcement legitimacy can be lost when tactics appear disproportionate to the underlying case.
