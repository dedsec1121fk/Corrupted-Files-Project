USA: Cambridge Analytica, Mass Data Harvesting, and Psychographic Election Targeting (2013-2025)

Status: [DOCUMENTED + STRONG EVIDENCE]

Summary:
Cambridge Analytica became the best-known case showing how commercial data extraction, political advertising, and behavioral profiling could merge. The strongest public record does not prove omnipotent "mind control," but it does prove deceptive harvesting of Facebook user information for political and commercial targeting.

What is documented:
- FTC's administrative complaint says the case sought to hold Cambridge Analytica responsible for deceptive acts used to harvest personal information from Facebook users for political and commercial targeted advertising.
- FTC's case summary says the company, its former CEO, and app developer Aleksandr Kogan used deceptive tactics to harvest personal information from tens of millions of Facebook users for voter profiling and targeting.
- FTC issued a final order after Cambridge Analytica failed to oppose the complaint on the merits.
- The scandal fed the broader realization that social-media platforms and data brokers could support political persuasion systems at a population scale.

Why it matters:
This is a cornerstone case for the modern "they manipulate people through data" fear. The dramatic versions of that claim are often overstated, but the core fear was not imaginary. Political microtargeting built on covertly harvested personal data was real.

What this archive does NOT claim:
- It does NOT claim Cambridge Analytica alone decided election outcomes.
- It does NOT claim psychographic scoring was all-powerful.
- It does claim the company and its partners crossed privacy and consent boundaries in service of political profiling.

Timeline:
- 2013-2014: Kogan's app gathers Facebook-linked data.
- 2016: Cambridge Analytica becomes globally infamous in political-data debates.
- 2018: Public backlash explodes after reporting and platform disclosures.
- 2019: FTC files complaint and later finalizes order against Cambridge Analytica; settlements also target Kogan and Alexander Nix.
- 2025: The case remains a reference point for election integrity, platform power, and data-harvesting debates.

Reading note:
This file belongs next to surveillance files because private-sector data extraction can become a quasi-intelligence capability when linked to politics.
