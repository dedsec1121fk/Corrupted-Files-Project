USA: Civil Asset Forfeiture, Equitable Sharing, and the "Policing for Profit" Structure (1984-2026)

Status: [DOCUMENTED]

Summary:
Civil asset forfeiture is a legal mechanism, not a hidden conspiracy. But it is part of this archive because it creates a documented structural incentive: agencies can seize assets tied to alleged criminal activity, route proceeds through forfeiture funds, and share proceeds with state and local partners. Critics call this "policing for profit." Even without adopting every criticism, the profit-linked structure itself is real and official.

What is documented:
- DOJ says the Asset Forfeiture Program includes seizure, forfeiture, and disposition of property tied to federal crimes.
- DOJ's Equitable Sharing Program allows participating state and local agencies to receive a share of federally forfeited assets.
- DOJ's FY 2027 budget narrative says about 6,000 state and local law-enforcement agencies participate in equitable sharing.
- The Supreme Court's Timbs v. Indiana decision underscored that civil forfeiture can collide with the Constitution's Excessive Fines Clause.
- DOJ OIG financial and program audits continue to track Asset Forfeiture Fund controls, reporting, and oversight.

Why it matters:
A system can be lawful and still create perverse incentives. Once agencies can financially benefit from seizures, the public has reason to question whether enforcement is always driven by neutral public safety priorities. That incentive structure is the core issue.

What this archive does NOT claim:
- It does NOT claim every forfeiture is abusive.
- It does NOT claim all participating agencies act corruptly.
- It does claim that the revenue-sharing architecture is real and that constitutional limits have had to be reasserted.

Timeline:
- 1984 onward: Modern federal forfeiture regime expands.
- 1990s-2010s: "Policing for profit" criticism grows.
- 2019: Timbs v. Indiana confirms the Excessive Fines Clause applies to the states.
- 2025-2026: DOJ continues publishing reports, budgets, and audit material for the Asset Forfeiture Fund and equitable sharing.

Reading note:
This is a good example of how a power system does not need to be secret to become dystopian. It only needs incentives that are misaligned with the rights of ordinary people.
