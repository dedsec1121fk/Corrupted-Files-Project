Mortgage crisis and foreclosure machine
=======================================

Country: USA
Year folder: 2007
Category: financial corruption / systemic failure
Evidence level: documented systemic failure

Core story
----------
The financial crisis exposed predatory lending, securitization abuse, rating failures and
foreclosure harms.

What happened, in full context
------------------------------
The crisis was not one villain but an ecosystem: mortgage brokers pushed risky loans, banks
packaged them, rating agencies blessed structures, regulators lagged, and homeowners carried
the damage. The scandal is that profits were privatized while collapse became public pain.

Timeline
--------
- 2007: mortgage market stress deepens.
- 2008: crisis becomes global.
- Aftermath: Dodd-Frank and settlements.

Key facts and evidence points
-----------------------------
- Subprime lending and mortgage-backed securities were central.
- Millions faced foreclosure.
- Bailouts and settlements followed.

Why this case matters
---------------------
It explains how technical finance can become a mass social disaster.

Competing explanations and caution
-----------------------------------
Some losses came from market risk; the documented misconduct and incentive failures were
still severe.

Source trail / suggested verification
-------------------------------------
