USA: Operation PBSUCCESS — CIA-Backed Coup in Guatemala (1952-1954)

1) A declassified covert operation, not a theory
Status: [DOCUMENTED]

Summary:
Operation PBSUCCESS is one of the best-known declassified examples of the CIA helping engineer regime change in another country.
The CIA's own reading-room material describes PBSUCCESS as the covert action that supported the 1954 coup in Guatemala.
The CIA FOIA Guatemala collection says it contains 5,120 documents and more than 14,000 pages covering CIA involvement in the 1954 coup.

Why it matters:
- It is a classic example of hidden U.S. intervention behind public anti-communist justification.
- It shows that “the government overthrows foreign governments in secret” is not fantasy.
- It demonstrates how intelligence agencies can shape another country's future while denying or minimizing their role publicly.

Key points:
- the target was the government of Jacobo Arbenz;
- the operation combined political warfare, covert action, and pressure rather than just open invasion;
- the later document trail became one of the landmark archival records for Cold War intervention.

Why it belongs here:
Many broad claims about U.S. foreign interference are too vague.
PBSUCCESS is the opposite: a documented case with an enormous declassified paper trail.

What it does NOT prove:
- It does not prove every later coup theory automatically.
- It does prove that covert regime-change operations were real, official, and historically consequential.
