USA: Location Data Brokers, Government Access Paths, and Car Surveillance (2024-2025)

1) Sensitive location data became a documented mass-market surveillance product
Status: [DOCUMENTED]
Summary:
A major “conspiracy theory come true” pattern in the modern U.S. is the merger of commercial surveillance and government access paths.
The recent FTC cases show that precise location data was being collected, sold, and reused in ways that could reveal visits to churches, clinics, military sites, labor unions, and homes. In parallel, the FTC also accused GM/OnStar of collecting and selling precise geolocation and driving-behavior data from millions of vehicles without adequately informed consent.

Why it matters:
This is a more modern surveillance model than the classic “government wiretap.”
Instead of one agency secretly watching everyone on its own, a market ecosystem collects intimate movement data first, then resells, profiles, or otherwise exposes it to downstream actors.
That is one of the strongest “connect-the-dots” patterns of the 2020s.

Key details:
- The FTC said Mobilewalla sold sensitive location data and harvested data from ad auctions at “a stunning scale.”
- The FTC said Gravy Analytics and Venntel unlawfully tracked and sold data about visits to sensitive sites, including health and religious locations, and did so for commercial and government uses without verifiable user consent.
- FTC staff explained that X-Mode allegedly sold location data to government contractors for national-security purposes.
- The FTC said GM and OnStar collected and sold precise geolocation and driving-behavior data from millions of vehicles and that some geolocation was collected as often as every three seconds.
- The FTC said some consumers later found their driving data had been used by insurers to set rates.

This file is not proof of one all-powerful secret system. It is evidence that the line between private-sector data extraction and state-style surveillance has become dangerously thin.
