Summary:
One of the strangest true entries in the archive is STARGATE: a real U.S. intelligence program that explored paranormal claims, especially “remote viewing,” for intelligence purposes.
CIA's STARGATE collection exists in the public reading room.
A declassified overview says the effort had main activity areas centered on remote viewing.
A later declassified evaluation says STARGATE was a Defense Intelligence Agency program involving paranormal phenomena, primarily remote viewing, for intelligence collection.

Why it matters:
- It proves that the U.S. government was willing to spend real time and resources testing claims that many people would call supernatural or absurd.
- It shows that “they secretly studied psychic spying” is not fiction.
- It also shows the difference between “a program existed” and “the claims were scientifically proven.”

What the evaluation record suggests:
The declassified evaluation of the remote-viewing program did not produce a strong case that it was a reliable intelligence tool.
So the reality is mixed:
- the paranormal research effort was real;
- the intelligence value appears to have been weak or unconvincing.

Why this file matters logically:
A lot of public arguments break down because people confuse three different things:
1. whether the government studied something;
2. whether the thing actually worked;
3. whether the government lied about having studied it.
STARGATE makes the first and third points very strong, even if the second point remains doubtful.
