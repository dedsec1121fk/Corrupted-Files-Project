My Lai massacre
===============

Country: USA
Year folder: 1968
Category: war crimes / military accountability
Evidence level: documented war crime

Core story
----------
US soldiers killed Vietnamese civilians in My Lai; the cover-up and limited accountability
became a defining Vietnam War scandal.

What happened, in full context
------------------------------
My Lai was not only a battlefield atrocity. It was also a chain-of-command and institutional
truth case. Civilians were killed, reports were suppressed or minimized, and the public
learned how military systems can protect reputation before victims. The eventual exposure
showed the importance of whistleblowers, journalists and congressional scrutiny.

Timeline
--------
- 1968: massacre occurs.
- 1969: public learns of the atrocity.
- 1970s: trials and public debate.

Key facts and evidence points
-----------------------------
- Hundreds of civilians were killed.
- The massacre was initially concealed.
- Only limited criminal accountability followed.

Why this case matters
---------------------
It is a core case for studying war crimes, dehumanization and command responsibility.

Competing explanations and caution
-----------------------------------
The killings are documented. Debate continues over how widespread similar conduct was and
how responsibility should have been assigned.

Source trail / suggested verification
-------------------------------------
- US National Archives: https://www.archives.gov/
