Sedition Act, war speech, raids and the fear state (1918-1921) COUNTRY: USA YEAR FOLDER: 1918 EVIDENCE LEVEL: documented/partly documented public record; disputed parts are labelled as disputed CATEGORY: Surveillance, secrecy and information control FULL ARTICLE / DOSSIER War emergency expanded the power to punish speech, organizing and dissent, creating an early American example of fear becoming policy. BACKGROUND The deeper mechanism in this type of case is secrecy plus asymmetry: one side collects, classifies, stores or shapes information while ordinary people cannot see the full system. The visible scandal is usually only the surface. Under it there are incentives, habits, institutions, budgets, files, contracts, social fears and political stories that existed before the public paid attention.
