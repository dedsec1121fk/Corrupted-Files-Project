Core case:
Protecting children online is a real goal. The question is whether age verification stays narrow or becomes a universal internet identity gate. The same mechanism that blocks harmful content for minors can identify adults before reading, speaking, organizing, or researching.

Documented ground:
Governments are reacting to teen mental health, addictive design, sexual exploitation, bullying, self-harm content, scams, and underage accounts. Families often feel platforms failed.

Claims, uncertainty, and evidence limits:
Document scans, facial estimation, app-store controls, and wallet IDs each carry different risks. The uncertainty is whether child protection remains limited or becomes general speech infrastructure.

Control risks to watch:
Watch for permanent browsing logs, age checks expanding to political or health content, biometric normalization, central private vendors, and no anonymous adult access.

How the reader can come to a conclusion:
The answer is not children versus freedom. Demand child-safety tools that minimize data, avoid document retention, use anonymous age tokens, and preserve the right to read anonymously.
