Palmer Raids and the first Red Scare
====================================

Country: USA
Year folder: 1919
Category: civil liberties / surveillance
Evidence level: documented government repression

Core story
----------
US authorities raided, detained and deported suspected radicals during panic over communism
and anarchism.

What happened, in full context
------------------------------
Attorney General A. Mitchell Palmer and federal agents used public fear after bombings and
labor unrest to justify mass raids. Many targets were immigrants or political radicals. The
case is a blueprint for how emergency rhetoric expands police power: labels first, raids
second, legal questions later.

Timeline
--------
- 1919: bomb scares and labor conflict intensify.
- 1919-1920: raids target radical networks.
- Afterward: civil-liberties backlash grows.

Key facts and evidence points
-----------------------------
- Thousands were arrested in raids.
- Deportations included political targets.
- The raids helped normalize federal domestic intelligence work.

Why this case matters
---------------------
This case helps explain later cycles of fear: communists, terrorists, migrants, gangs,
hackers, extremists.

Competing explanations and caution
-----------------------------------
Some radicals did advocate violence, but the raids swept far wider than proven criminal
conduct and damaged due process.
